# Three corners, three glyphs — how far in, and why

**R-MAC-CORNERS**, 2026-09-13. Research only; no product code changes. Strict
mock: `scratchpad/mock-mac-corners.html` — each corner at 4×, candidates side by
side, real captures beside them.

## What was measured

Three windows, captured read-only on the Mac mini (macOS 26.6.2) by window id
with `screencapture -x -o -l<id>`, which leaves the rounded corner transparent —
the window's own mask arrives in the alpha channel. Captures are 2×; numbers are
points. The radius is read off the 45° diagonal: the gap `D` from the corner
pixel to the first alpha at or above 50%, where a circular arc of radius `r` has
`D = (√2 − 1)·r`. All four corners of each window measured equal.

| window | size | D | circle-equivalent r | arc extent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finder (Apple, macOS 26 toolbar window) | 920 × 464 | 10.25 | 24.75 | 31.0 |
| **Folio** (winit, titled + full-size content view) | 960 × 600 | 6.72 | **16.22** | 17.0 |
| NetEase Music (third party, custom frame) | 1061 × 752 | 4.63 | 11.18 | — |

**The ticket's "10–12 pt" is a version behind.** That is what a borderless window
gets, not what Folio's gets. Apple's own line from WWDC25 is that the radius now
*varies based on the style of window* — and on one desktop, one OS, one minute
apart, it does. Folio's window is **16.2**. The corners are continuous rather
than circular (extent 17.0 against a circle-equivalent 16.2), but the diagonal is
where this is decided and the circle-equivalent is exact there.

| traffic-light left edges, ⌀14 | 19 / 42 / 65 | 9 / 32 / 55 |
| first light centre, from the left / top | 26.0 / 25.0 | 16.0 / 20.0 |
| last top-right control, ink centre from the right | 25.0 | 23.0 |

## The geometry

Put the corner at the origin; the arc has radius `r`, centred at `(r, r)`. A
glyph's **ink** box stands `p` in from one edge and `q` in from the other.

```
clearance to the straight edges  =  min(p, q)
clearance to the arc             =  r − √((r−p)² + (r−q)²)      [only when p < r and q < r]
a disc instead, centre (cx, cy), radius ρ
                                 =  r − √((r−cx)² + (r−cy)²) − ρ
```

Ask that the arc never be the tightest neighbour:
`r − √((r−p)² + (r−q)²) ≥ min(p, q)`. On the diagonal (`p = q`) that reduces to
`p ≥ r`; off it, with `q < r`, it reduces to `p ≥ r` as well — the inequality has
no interior solution. Two riders: measure **ink**, not nominal boxes (a 14 pt
gear glyph paints 11 × 12 of it), and a disc is far more forgiving in a corner
than a square of the same size, which is why Apple's own corner glyph is round.

## The rule

> **A glyph standing in a window corner keeps its ink at least as far from the
> arc as from the nearer straight edge. For anything with a square shoulder that
> means one of its two insets reaches `r` — 16 pt on the window Folio ships on
> macOS 26. A disc satisfies it for free once its centre passes the arc's centre
> line on either axis.**

Calibrate against the close button's own clearance, 9.00 pt: not a guideline
number — Apple publishes none — but the placement nobody argues with.

## The three glyphs

| glyph | placement | p, q (ink) | arc gap | clearance | proposal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| close button | disc ⌀14, centre (16, 20) | 9, 13 | 9.44 | 9.00 edge | unchanged |
| gear | centre 23 from the right, 20 down | 17.5, 14 | clear | 14.00 edge | 23 → 16, as planned (10.09 arc) |
| files foot mark | 13 pt box, pad 12, 7 up | 13, 9 | **8.32** | **8.32 arc** | pad 12 → **16** (9.00 edge) |

**The close button — leave it, and hear one thing.** Its centre sits 20 down,
past the arc's centre line at 16.2, so its nearest boundary is the straight left
edge: 9.00, the same 9 it would have on a square window. But **Finder's lights
are not where Folio's are**. Apple, on this same OS, puts them at 19 / 42 / 65,
first centre (26, 25); Folio's 9 / 32 / 55 is Folio's own placement (§13.20 sets
those frames), inherited from before macOS 26. Keeping it is defensible — the
lights must centre on the 40 pt band and share the pill's axis — but *that is
where Apple puts them* is no longer true, and is now a decision rather than a
fact.

**The gear — T-MAC-STRIP-ENDS is safe.** Centred in the last 46 pt caption box,
the gear's centre lands 23 in, which by arithmetic nobody intended *is* the
diagonal-corrected answer (`p = 17.5`, past `r`). Moving it to 16 to mirror the
close button hands the arc the tightest place, but only down to 10.09 — still a
point clear of the light's 9.00, because the gear's ink is round-shouldered.
Apple makes the same mirror in Finder: its last toolbar control's ink centre sits
25.0 from the right against the first light's 26.0 from the left. (`feature/macos-strip-ends` is still at `main`; nothing pushed to read.)

**The files foot — the only glyph the corner bites.** The bar is 28 tall and the
mark centred in the 27 below its hairline, so the bottom inset is fixed at 7 (9
of ink) by a constant the preview pane shares: `x` is the only free axis. At pad
12 the ink box stands (13, 9) from the corner, arc gap 8.32 — under the close
button's 9.00 and under its own 9 to the floor. Raising `FILES_FOOT_PADDING_X`
to 16 puts `p` past `r`, takes the arc out of the running and lands the clearance
on 9.00. The trap is the other mirror: the light's 16 pt *centre* means pad 9.5,
which moves the mark **towards** the arc and costs another 1.4.

**Finder is not a model at that corner.** Its bottom-left belongs to the sidebar,
whose rows keep their flat 28 pt inset and are simply *clipped* by the arc — the
last row in the capture is cut in half. That is a scroller running under the
corner: right for a list, wrong for fixed furniture. Finder's path bar, the real
counterpart of Folio's foot, spans only the content pane, so it never meets a
corner while the sidebar is open. Unverified: where its first crumb sits with the
sidebar hidden — toggling it would have altered the owner's window.

## Tradeoffs

The +4 belongs in a derivation, not a fourth constant: the extra inset is
`max(0, r − pad)` on whichever axis is free, applied only where a glyph stands in
a live rounded corner — the foot only when its column is leftmost and its bar
reaches the window floor. On Windows it is a no-op, the 8 px corner already being
under every one of these insets, so no second code path appears. The cost is four
points of path text in the narrowest column; the cost of doing nothing is the one
glyph in the window sitting closer to the glass than the close button does.

## Sources

* Measurements: `scratchpad/corners/{finder,folio,netease}.png`, 2×, this
  machine, 2026-09-13. Every number in the tables is from those.
* Apple publishes **no** numeric window corner radius, traffic-light inset or
  toolbar height in the current guidelines: the Windows, Toolbars and Layout
  pages were fetched and carry none. Every figure in circulation is
  reverse-engineering, and none is cited here.
* Apple, WWDC25 session 356: shapes are fixed, capsule or **concentric**, the
  last a radius *"calculated by subtracting padding from parent"*, and *"each
  element is designed with a curvature that sits neatly within the corner radius
  of its container"*. Read from a transcript, not Apple's own page — secondhand.
* The practitioner form of the same rule — inner radius = outer radius − padding,
  valid only where the two shapes share a centre, otherwise *"the gap swells or
  collapses at 45°"* — is the concentric-border-radius write-up on dev.to. The
  formula above restates it for a shape with no radius of its own: a square
  shoulder is the zero-radius limit, which can satisfy concentricity only by
  standing clear of the arc.
* Nothing usable for Arc, Raycast, Things or Kitty; the one real third-party
  number is a Ghostty discussion settling on 8 pt of content padding to line up
  with the native lights. No app surveyed puts anything in a bottom-left corner,
  which makes Folio's foot unusual rather than wrong.

## Correction, 2026-09-13 (later the same day)

The shipping window, captured by window id with `screencapture -x -o -l` from a throwaway instance of the owner's own build (dark theme, 2×, 960×600 pt), measures a corner radius of **12.1 pt** by the same diagonal method (`D` = 10 px = 5.0 pt), not the 16.2 above, which came from a probe window. The strip (40.0), the lights (14.0 pt, centres x 16 / 39 / 62, y 20) match the strict mock. At r = 12.1 the files-column foot glyph (ink inset 13) is **not** bitten by the arc, so the `FILES_FOOT_PADDING_X` 12 → 16 change is withdrawn unless a later measurement of the shipping window says otherwise; the gear at 16 stays endorsed (its inset exceeds r).
