## 0. The ruling this is written under, and the framing it revises twice

**The owner's direction, 2026-09-15: "modular, reusable, pluggable, with
well-designed interfaces; plan first; Codex reviews and improves the plan."**

The framing put to the owner before any measurement was: follow the seams the
workspace already has — `bt-term`, `bt-viewport`, `bt-render`, `bt-math`,
`bt-doc`, `bt-platform`, `bt-persist` are already separate crates — the disease
is the god object `Runtime` in `bt-app`, and the goal is a thin orchestrator over
subsystems behind narrow interfaces. Not a third-party plugin system.

**The shape of that framing survives. Its diagnosis was wrong twice, and each
correction cost something the plan had already promised.**

1. **`Runtime` is a two-field struct, but it is not a narrow facade.**
   `struct Runtime<'a> { app: &'a mut App, window: &'a mut WindowRuntime }`, and
   the crate pins that shape with a `const _` destructure. But it also
   implements `Deref<Target = TabState>` and `DerefMut` (`main.rs:16477`,
   `:16485`), so it hands out the active tab implicitly as well; and methods
   such as `answer_attention` (`:92786`) destructure `WindowRuntime` and mutate
   `tabs[index]` without ever writing `self.window.tabs`. The god objects are
   `App` (78 fields) and `WindowRuntime` (245 fields), 1,310 methods hang off
   `impl Runtime`, and **any conclusion about who owns what that was drawn from
   a field-name regex is a lower bound, not an ownership proof** (review
   finding 5). §3 keeps the census and withdraws the conclusions.

2. **The obstacle to extracting subsystems is the module cycle, and it is far
   worse than the first draft said.** Measured with items owned by `main.rs`
   resolved into a single `@root` node, the largest strongly connected component
   of `bt-app` is **80 nodes and 442,903 lines** — not the 43 nodes and 232,495
   lines the first draft reported, which counted only `crate::module::` edges
   and let a module that names a root-owned type look free. Both numbers are
   real measurements of different graphs; **`root_prod` is the one a crate split
   has to obey**, because a new crate cannot name a type that lives in the
   binary it was cut out of.

3. **So the extractable surface is 6.9% of the crate, not 14.5%.** After Step 1's
   cut, **22 modules and 31,358 lines** can leave with no root-owned item
   relocated first. The first draft claimed 51 modules and 66,273 lines. What
   survives intact is the *argument* for Step 1: the multiplier the `i18n` cut
   buys is 2.67× on the root-aware graph against 2.70× on the first one. What
   does not survive is the absolute mass, and everything §10 estimated from it.

**The plan therefore gains a step it did not have and loses a promise it should
not have made.** The step is Step 1, three functions in `i18n.rs`. The promise
is Step 3's size: sixteen crates become **thirteen rows over 22 modules**, and a
second table now names, per blocked module, the root-owned item that has to move
before it can follow.

## 1. The problem, in numbers

*Measured; inventory §§1–3. Where the review's independent count differs from
the first draft's, the review's is used and the inventory records both.*

- `bt-app` is **456,556 lines — 67.3% of all first-party Rust under `crates/`**
  (678,745). The first draft's 65.6% divided by a denominator that included
  `vendor/`.
- `crates/bt-app/src` is **102 files, 455,336 physical lines**. `main.rs` is
  **165,815** physical lines, of which **63,487 (38%) are two `impl Runtime<'_>`
  blocks** holding 1,310 methods. Five methods — `mouse_input`,
  `refresh_chrome`, `chrome_mouse_input`, `keyboard_input`, `create` — are 4,858
  lines between them.
- **43.3% of the crate is `#[cfg(test)]`** — 197,346 lines by the review's
  cfg-inheriting count; `main.rs` alone 59,903. **3,809 syntactic `#[test]`
  attributes**, 1,043 of them in `main.rs`, 770 of those in a *single* `mod
  tests` of 46,636 lines. None of these is a libtest census; a `#[test]` behind
  a platform `cfg` is counted here and may not run.
- `bt-app` has **no `[lib]` target**, so an integration test cannot reach its
  items and all its tests compile into **one** harness. (`crates/bt-app/tests/`
  does exist — it holds the `harness = false` macOS glyph target and a
  `fixtures/` directory. The constraint is "no library to link against", not
  "no `tests/` directory".)
- Artifact sizes from the owner's own earlier builds, **historical observations
  and not reproducible in this worktree**: product bin 85.8 MiB; unit-test
  harness 209.8 MiB with a **654.6 MiB PDB**; `target\debug` 74.5 GiB.
- `main.rs` grew **+34,764 lines in the fifteen days** from `v0.1.0-preview` to
  HEAD.
- **112 of the last 150 `--no-merges` commits that touch `crates/bt-app` also
  touch `main.rs` — 75%.** The first draft said 98 of 150 and that number
  reproduces under no reading of `git log`; the four readings and the exact
  command are in inventory §2.3. The same pass gives `seats.rs` 29, `i18n.rs`
  24, `preview.rs` 17, `settings.rs` 12. **The correction makes §6.5's freeze
  argument stronger, not weaker.**

The consequences the owner feels — a 60–90 minute gate, batches that conflict,
agents navigating a god object — are downstream of those six lines.

**One number the first draft used is withdrawn entirely.** It said 58% of the
crate is code and "the remaining 42% is prose", and divided estimates by 1.7×
accordingly. The complement of the 263,947 code lines includes blank lines and
string literals as well as comments, so neither the description nor the factor
is sound. No estimate in this revision uses it.

## 3. The state census, and what may and may not be concluded from it

The first draft called this "the interface map" and drew ownership conclusions
from it. Finding 5 disproves those conclusions. **What is kept is the census;
what is withdrawn is every sentence that turned a field count into a boundary.**

### 3.1 The census

*Measured, lexically.* For every one of the 1,310 methods, which
`self.window.<field>` and `self.app.<field>` names appear in its body:

- `WindowRuntime` has 245 fields; **242** are reached by direct access allowing
  whitespace (239 by contiguous match). `App` has 78; **78** are reached.
- Of the 239 contiguously-reached `WindowRuntime` fields, **86 are touched by
  exactly one theme** and **38 by five or more**.
- The worst: `renderer` (220 methods / 25 themes), `tabs` (233 / 23 —
  **255 allowing whitespace**), `active_tab` (154 / 21), `window` (79 / 21),
  `app.motion` (90 / 18), `app.settings_store` (84 / 20), `app.gpu` (64 / 21).

### 3.2 What that is evidence of, and what it is not

**It is evidence of** where state is concentrated: three fields — `tabs`,
`active_tab`, `window` — are read by most of the crate, and the three service
objects behind them are read far more often than written. That much is safe.

**It is not evidence of ownership, and not of a one-way dependency**, for three
reasons the review names:

- `Runtime: Deref<Target = TabState>`, so any method may reach the active tab
  with no `self.window.` prefix at all. A regex counting field access cannot see
  it.
- `answer_attention` (`main.rs:92786`) destructures `WindowRuntime` and mutates
  `tabs[index]`. The census records that method as not touching `tabs`.
- A field *name* appearing in a body says nothing about read versus write, and
  nothing about what the method's callees do with the `&mut Runtime` they were
  handed.

**So the first draft's table of "genuinely one-way subsystems" is withdrawn.**
Seven themes were listed as already one-way; the review's finding 14 shows
`attention`, `search`, `first run`, `quake`, `math`, `palette` and `diagnostics`
each mutating state the table said they did not touch. §8 no longer promises
them.

**What replaces it** is a smaller, defensible statement, and it is what Step 4
starts from rather than what Step 4 concludes:

> The three hub fields — `tabs`, `active_tab`, `window` — are the document
> model. No boundary that leaves them on the far side of an interface will hold,
> and no interface that hands a subsystem `&mut tabs` is a boundary. Everything
> else is an open question until a read/write/call audit answers it.

## 4. Step 0 — the free fix, the baseline, and the profile trials

**Independently landable. Re-ordered by the review: the first item is free,
independent of the whole split, and can land the same afternoon.**

### 4.1 First: make the shortcut scripts select what the gate selects

*Finding 17. This is the "third compile", and it is not a sequencing accident —
the first draft's guess was wrong and its proposed `Measure-Command` would have
confirmed the wrong cause.*

`scripts/check-shortcuts-table.ps1:31` and `scripts/generate-shortcuts-table.ps1:32`
run `cargo test --package bt-app --bin folio --locked`, while the gate runs
`cargo test --workspace --locked`. The two resolve **different features**:

```
--workspace : vte v0.15.0 | ansi,bitflags,cursor-icon,default,log,serde,std
-p bt-app   : vte v0.15.0 | ansi,bitflags,cursor-icon,      log,serde,std
```

`default` arrives because `bt-corpus` — a workspace member selected by
`--workspace` and not by `-p bt-app` — takes `vte.workspace = true` without
`default-features = false`. Features reach rustc as `--cfg feature="…"` and are
part of the unit fingerprint, so `vte` is a different unit under the two
commands. `vte` is under the vendored `alacritty_terminal`, under `bt-term`,
under `bt-app` — **so everything above it rebuilds, including the 209.8 MiB
harness and its link, every time the shortcuts gate runs.** It buys nothing:
`vte`'s `default` is `["std"]` and `std` is already active in both resolutions.

**The change**, in both scripts, is one line:

```powershell
cargo test --workspace --locked -- --exact shortcuts::tests::docs_shortcuts_md_is_the_bindings_table
```

Neither script runs in CI — no `.github/workflows` file names either — so this
is entirely the owner's local gate, which is the gate §10 is about. Keep the
ordering rule too (run the scripts after the gate's `cargo test`, same tree,
same target directory) and write it into `CONTRIBUTING.md`; it is worth having,
it is just not what was costing the compile.

### 4.2 Second: the baseline

Everything in §10 is a model and should not be trusted a day longer than it
takes to replace. Run this **after** §4.1, so that what it measures is
sequencing rather than the feature difference.

Three cases, three runs each, on the owner's machine in a worktree with its own
`target`: **cold** (dependencies unbuilt), **warm with no edit**, and
**app-only edit** (touch one line of `main.rs`).

For each: `cargo build --workspace --locked`, `cargo test --workspace --locked
--no-run`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --locked -- -D warnings`, and
the suite run. Record, per run: **the `--timings` JSON kept**, `-v` output so the
*unit set* each command compiled is on the record (which is the quantity the
rest of this plan argues about), the target triple, the toolchain, the effective
flags, `jobs`, the resolved feature set, peak rustc and linker RSS, the link
step alone (the `--timings` HTML names it), and suite wall-clock.

### 4.3 Third: `opt-level`, as an artifact-sharing measurement

*Finding 18, which re-frames this item and promotes it. The first draft ranked
it third and called it a test-runtime trade; it is neither.*

`[profile.test] opt-level = 1` is at `Cargo.toml:193–194`. **There is no
`[profile.dev]` in this workspace** — the only `^[profile` lines are 193 and 199
— so dev is the built-in default at `opt-level = 0`. A profile applies to every
target and reaches every dependency, so `cargo test` compiles the entire graph
at `1` while `cargo build` and `cargo run` compile the same graph at `0`. **The
two share nothing**, and the owner's loop is build, run, test on one machine.

The measurement is therefore not "is the suite slower at 0". It is *what does
the gate cost when `cargo test` can reuse what `cargo run` just built*: three
runs of `cargo test --workspace --locked --no-run` immediately after a green
`cargo build --workspace --locked`, at `1` and at `0`.

If the suite does need optimised dependencies, the setting that keeps both
properties is the override rather than the blanket key:

```toml
[profile.dev.package."*"]
opt-level = 1

[profile.test.package."*"]
opt-level = 1
```

— with **no** blanket `[profile.test] opt-level`, so workspace code is `0` under
both commands, dependencies are `1` under both, and the flags match unit for
unit. Whether `[profile.test]` inherits `[profile.dev]`'s override table or
needs its own copy is the one thing the trial must settle by reading
`cargo test -v`: write both and check. Whatever is chosen, the measured numbers
go in the manifest beside the key, the way `[profile.release]` already does.

**The risk this item carries is a clock, not a correctness failure.** The
robustness child at `main.rs:116321` has a 60-second process watchdog, a
30-second font warm-up and 1/5/10-second request budgets; an unoptimised Typst
conversion can trip them without changing any functional output. Record the
child's measured margin against all four budgets on the Windows CI runner as
well as on the owner's machine.

### 4.4 Fourth: `debug = "line-tables-only"`, aimed at the PDB

*Finding 19. The first draft aimed this at the 209.8 MiB harness. It will not
move it.*

This repository has already measured the size question from both sides, in its
own manifest: `strip = "symbols"` produced a `folio.exe` of *exactly* the same
size, "because MSVC never put the symbols in the executable — they are in
`folio.pdb`" (`Cargo.toml:212`); and adding line tables to release moved
`folio.pdb` 9.94 MB → 139.09 MB while `folio.exe` moved by 94,208 bytes
(`:236`, `:239`).

So the target is **the 654.6 MiB PDB and the link's debug-info work**, not the
image. The harness image is code: the ~124 MiB by which it exceeds the 85.8 MiB
bin is the crate's own test code plus `libtest`, and `bt-render`/`winit`
monomorphisation is in both images, so it is not what makes the harness larger.
**§10 and §7.3 are two separate wins on two separate quantities and are no longer
added together.** State the expected win in PDB bytes and link seconds, and
measure the link alone.

**And it is not confined to test backtraces.** Because the selected profile
applies to every target, `cargo test` also builds the `folio` **bin** under
`[profile.test]`, and cargo uplifts it to the same `target\debug\folio.exe` that
`cargo build`'s dev-profile bin uses. The first draft's "the risk is confined to
test backtraces" is true of the shipped release binary and false of the
executable a developer has on disk after a gate. The ticket must say which
`target\debug\folio.exe` a panic or hang investigation may be run against.

**Before this item changes any debug setting, the guard has to exist.**
*Finding 20*: `panic_report` (`main.rs:115969`) formats
`"panic: {panic_text}\nbacktrace:\n{backtrace}"`, and the only production-shaped
panic test in the crate asserts nothing but
`read_to_string(log).contains("unwrap")` (`:116432`) — which an unwrap panic
satisfies from the `panic:` line alone, with the backtrace section empty,
unresolved or absent. `hang_watch`'s report tests use synthetic frames
(`hang_watch.rs:2403`), not a real PDB. **So no Step 0 profile change can make
any existing test go red.** Add an assertion that the backtrace section resolves
at least one frame to a named function in this crate, and prove it fires by
building once with `debug = false`.

### 4.5 Last: the linker trial

*Finding 7, which corrects the mechanics, and the review's §4.3 item 4, which
corrects the citation.*

`rust-lld.exe` is present in the pinned toolchain — **`1.94.1-x86_64-pc-windows-msvc`
from `rust-toolchain.toml`**, not the 1.89 MSRV the first draft cited, and it is
that toolchain's `rust-lld` and PDB writer the trial measures.

**There is no `[profile.test] linker` key.** Cargo selects a linker *by target*.
A persistent `[target.x86_64-pc-windows-msvc]` override in `.cargo/config.toml`
would reach Windows **release** builds as well, so the trial must scope the
override to its own command environment. Two further traps:

- `.cargo/config.toml:39` carries `target-feature=+crt-static`. Replacing
  `RUSTFLAGS` wholesale silently discards it; Linux CI deliberately overrides it
  the other way (`ci.yml:624`). Compose, do not replace.
- Do not apply MSVC linker configuration to the macOS or Linux jobs.

Record the MSVC driver and flavor, the PDB identity, and that symbol resolution
still works — then decide. And assess Windows build scripts and native libraries,
not only the harness.

**Tests that guard Step 0:** the three gates, plus the new backtrace assertion of
§4.4 and the recorded budget margins of §4.3.
**Rollback:** revert the manifest and script lines. No source moved.
**Interaction with 0.4.1:** §4.1 touches two scripts and nothing else; the rest
touches the manifest. None of it touches `crates/`.

## 6. Step 2 — `main.rs` into `runtime/*.rs`, by theme

**Independently landable. Zero behaviour change in the *product*. Days.**

The review's verdict is *go with changes* for both halves. The changes are
substantial and they all point the same way: **this step is not the purely
mechanical move the first draft described, and pretending it is would put a
visibility review, a platform-array edit and 91 source pins inside a commit
whose only cheap verification is "nothing changed".**

### 6.1 What moves

Rust allows `impl Runtime<'_>` blocks in as many files of one crate as you like,
and an item declared private at the crate root stays visible in every descendant
module — so `runtime::preview` can `use crate::{Runtime, WindowRuntime,
TabState};` and touch private fields exactly as `main.rs` does today.

**But private *inherent methods* do not work that way** (finding 9). A private
method moved into one child module is not reachable from its parent or from a
sibling. `open_search` (`main.rs:42046`) calls `refresh_chrome` (`:39271`) and
the two land in different themes; there are many such pairs. **The ticket
therefore explicitly permits and reviews the minimum `pub(crate)` or
`pub(in crate::runtime)` on every method called outside its new module**, keeps
narrow helper visibility everywhere else, and lists the widened set for review.
`use super::*` in a sibling test module does not restore access to a sibling's
private definitions.

**And the first draft's arithmetic was wrong.** It said 2a leaves "roughly
40,000 lines" in `main.rs`. Its own numbers give **165,815 − 63,487 = 102,329**
before any module or import lines are added; ~40k is a possible *post-2b* scale.
Free functions, `static`/`const` declarations, trait impls (`main.rs:16477`
supplies one outside both blocks) and shared fixtures each need a named
destination. **The ticket carries a relocation manifest, item by item, not a
theme list.**

Proposed shape, unchanged in spirit: `runtime/mod.rs` plus about twenty-six
theme files, the largest ~9,000 lines (preview) and most under 2,500.

### 6.2 The three pieces of work that are *not* relocation

These are the reason the step is not mechanical, and — per the review's risk
ordering — **each should be its own reviewed commit *before* the freeze window
opens**, so that the frozen window contains only text relocation.

**(a) `FILES_THAT_MAY_NAME_A_PLATFORM` must be edited** (finding 16). The first
draft said the array stays in `main.rs` and the script is untouched, "the
cheapest possible answer". It is not available. The array is at
`main.rs:163012`, inside `#[cfg(test)] mod platform_gate_tests`, and one of its
eleven entries is `"main.rs"` itself — admitted for the startup path and the
five platform calls M1-1 made non-fatal, which §6.1 proposes to move into
`runtime/launch.rs`. Its test,
`only_the_named_files_decide_what_platform_this_is` (`:163103`), walks
`sources()` (`:163045`) **recursively**, producing `/`-normalised relative
names, so a moved file arrives as `runtime/launch.rs`; and it asserts in **both**
directions — `strangers` empty (a new file naming a platform fails) *and*
`silent` empty (a listed name that has stopped naming one fails). Moving any
platform `cfg` out of `main.rs` trips both at once.
`scripts/check-portable-core.ps1` parses the array out of `main.rs` by regex
(`:227`), requires at least five entries (`:240`), and at `:248` does the same
recursive walk against the same relative name.

So: **the ticket owns the array.** Enumerate which platform `cfg` lines travel
with which theme, rewrite the array and its `[&str; 11]` arity in the same
commit, and — per `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s "a new guard proves it fires" — plant a
platform `cfg` in a new `runtime/*.rs` file and watch both the Rust twin and the
PowerShell script go red before it lands.

Note the asymmetry beside it: `the_shell_page_is_gone` (`main.rs:161410`) uses a
**non-recursive** `read_dir` of the same directory, so adding `runtime/` silently
removes every moved file from that whole-program guard. The two guards in one
file disagree about what "the crate" is, and 2a must fix the second as
deliberately as it edits the first.

**(b) The 91 source pins, and the first draft's account of them was wrong**
(phase-5 question 4). **None of the 91 uses `source_pin::source_region`.** That
helper's three users are `first_run.rs`, `profiles.rs` and
`shell_integration.rs`; `source_region(` appears zero times in `main.rs`. All 91
declare a `const SOURCE: &str = include_str!("main.rs")` — 87 named `SOURCE`,
two `MAIN`, two `source`; 38 at module level inside a test module and 53 inside
a function, so **they are 91 macro invocations, not 91 independent tests** — and
slice it with hand-rolled `body(signature)` helpers that
`panic!("{signature} is declared in this file")` when the header is gone.

That failure mode is loud, and it is the argument *for* re-pointing rather than
rewriting. The genuinely silent class is small and enumerable: **three**
`!SOURCE.contains(…)` assertions (`main.rs:15772`, `:103570`, `:105705`) pass
vacuously on a fragment that no longer contains their subject. Seven more are
`SOURCE.matches(..).count() == N` with non-zero `N` and fail loudly.

So: **re-point the ~88 loud pins** at the file their subject moved to, and
**convert the three negative ones to an explicit whole-crate walk** — those are
the ones a PowerShell gate would be right for, and `check-portable-core.ps1` is
the working example. A multi-file pin already exists in the tree
(`main.rs:165696` reads `main.rs` and `preview_edit.rs`), so the
`const SOURCES: &[&str]` form has precedent. The first draft's risk-table entry
crediting `source_region`'s panic as the guard for all 91 is struck: that guard
is not installed in any of them.

**(c) Every other source-reading and exact-path consumer** (finding 10). The
child process at `main.rs:116325` runs `current_exe()` with the **hard-coded
exact path** `tests::hostile_math_is_refused_and_the_real_decoration_worker_survives`.
2b changes that path. A zero-match libtest run **returns success**, and the
parent checks only the exit status — so the guard evaporates while the top-level
test still appears in `--list` under its new name. The ticket updates the
selector **and** requires proof the child body ran: an explicit completion
marker, or a verified one-test census.

### 6.3 How it is verified, and what the checks cannot do

*Finding 11. The first draft's two mechanical checks are necessary and they are
not sufficient; it half-admitted this and then relied on them anyway.*

- **`--list` is a topology check, not a behaviour check.** A byte-identical list
  proves no test was lost or renamed in 2a. It cannot detect a changed method
  body. For 2b, use a **one-to-one map of old target + full test identity to new
  identity**, carrying cfg and `#[ignore]` status and multiplicity — leaf-name
  sets alone do not prove preserved coverage. Where a whole test module can stay
  at its old logical path, `#[path]` preserves it and should.
- **The sorted-multiset line diff is replaced.** Sorting loses control-flow
  order, so swapping two effects passes it; and it *rejects* the visibility,
  pin and platform-array edits that §6.1 and §6.2 show are mandatory. Replace it
  with a comparison of **each relocated item's parsed token body, in order**,
  against a reviewed allow-list for path and visibility changes.
- **Run rustfmt over the new module tree and review formatting separately.**
  Changing indentation and import position changes rustfmt's output; that must
  not arrive mixed into the relocation diff.
- **Re-check what the move can change without changing a token**: cfg-scoped
  imports, inherited lint attributes, dead-code exposure, and clippy on both
  supported app platforms. CI reality, corrected: macOS checks `bt-app` with
  `--all-targets` (`ci.yml:517`); **Linux explicitly excludes `bt-app`**
  (`:644`, portable list at `:649`). The existing Linux job does not validate an
  app split.
- **Keep the full behavioural gate per landing.** The three gates, plus
  `check-portable-core.ps1` and `check-shortcuts-table.ps1`.

### 6.4 Expected effect

**Compile time: zero to very slightly negative, and the plan says so.** Type
checking, name resolution and macro expansion are per-crate; incremental
compilation is keyed on items and queries, not files; and CI sets
`CARGO_INCREMENTAL: 0`. Twenty-six modules add a little name-resolution and
metadata work. The review independently confirms this reading.

What it buys: **navigation** (the handoff already instructs agents not to read
the file but to grep it, because it cannot be read) and **merges** — at 112 of
150 commits, a preview ticket and a git-panel ticket currently land in the same
file and after the split they do not. It is also the precondition for §8.

**Struck:** the first draft offered "Step 2 may fix `cargo test --release`" as a
benefit. `cargo test --release` selects `[profile.release]` with `lto = "fat"`
and `codegen-units = 1`; neither depends on file count, and rustc's stack
exhaustion is per-item recursion, which splitting a file does not make shallower
for any single item (finding 23). Two cheaper candidates should be tried first
and on their own: **`RUST_MIN_STACK` raised for the rustc process**, and a
**custom profile inheriting release with `lto = false, codegen-units = 16`** for
optimised test runs.

### 6.5 The landing window, risks, rollback

**The freeze is right and the review's own arithmetic strengthens it.** At 112
of 150 commits touching `main.rs`, essentially every open branch has to be
rebased against the split. One freeze costs each open branch **one** rebase;
twenty-six independently landed theme commits cost each open branch up to
twenty-six, because each moves lines out from under it again — and 26
independently gated landings cost 26–39 hours of gate time by themselves at the
reported 60–90 minutes.

**So: keep the freeze, and make the window short rather than the landings
small.** Concretely:

1. Land §6.2's three pieces — the platform array, the 88 re-pointed pins and the
   three converted ones, the child-process selector — as their own reviewed
   commits, **before** the freeze.
2. Inventory the branches actually open at dispatch and record the source commit
   and the ownership window. The in-tree handoff records 0.4.1 math UI,
   input/TSF and card work, all of which overlap the proposed themes. **112 of
   150 is a historical frequency, not a probability for the branches that are
   actually open** — look, do not infer.
3. Open the window. Inside it: text relocation only, reviewed as one commit per
   theme, merged as one batch.
4. Rebase each affected branch once on the completed split and re-run its tests.

**Rollback** is a revert of the batch, with one caveat the first draft missed: a
later revert can conflict with feature edits that landed after it even when the
original patch was a pure move. And revert the **exact** lockfile delta —
`cargo update -w` is not a guarantee of restoring the original resolution.

## 8. Step 4 — the orchestrator boundary: not yet

**Verdict: rethink; do not dispatch. This section is a problem statement and a
list of prerequisites. It is not a design, and the first draft's sketch is
withdrawn.**

### 8.1 Why the sketch was withdrawn

The first draft offered a `Services` / `Turn` / `Intent` sketch and named seven
subsystems as "genuinely one-way today". Finding 14 disproves the second claim
for every one of the seven, from the code:

| Claimed one-way subsystem | What it actually does |
| --- | --- |
| attention / notifications | `answer_attention` mutates a tab's ledger through destructuring (`main.rs:92786`); `mark_attention_seen` mutates every leaf (`:92814`); `raise_attention` calls app notification services (`:81853`) |
| search | `seat_can_search` reads sessions through `Runtime`'s `Deref` (`:41989`); `refresh_search` branches between transcript and WebView with deliberate keyboard-focus behaviour (`:42172`) |
| first run / integration | `answer_first_run` mutates card state, applies settings and closes and rebuilds UI synchronously (`:52351`) |
| quake / summon | `show_quake_window` reads app settings, quake state and monitor DPI, then places and presents a real window (`:38061`) |
| math / formula | `apply_math_results` routes by owner and `LeafId` and mutates frozen and live sessions (`:81271`); stale and closed panes are deliberately ignored |
| command palette | `run_palette_row` closes the palette before dispatch, then resolves stable tab and seat ids against current state (`:93538`) |
| diagnostics / trace | the panic hook and hang watchdog have mandatory production behaviour (`:115786`, `hang_watch.rs:1386`) |

And the sketch's own material problem, independent of those: **deferred intents
have no identity, ordering, error or publication contract.** `turn`
(`main.rs:99848`) currently requires wheel flush, deferred-DPI reconciliation,
result application and PTY drain **in that order**; draining a `Vec<Intent>`
later changes what subsequent work sees.

### 8.2 What "pluggable" may mean here, now that one meaning is gone

*Finding 22.* No first-party crate in this workspace declares a `[features]`
table — the only one in the tree is `vendor/alacritty_terminal/Cargo.toml:40`.
`crates/bt-app/src` contains no `cfg(feature` at all, and CI has no
feature-matrix job. **"Compiled out" is not implementable today**, and
implementing it means a second build configuration the three gates do not cover
— which is the exact shape of the incident `CONVENTIONS.md` records.

So the word is redefined, once, and the plan uses only this:

> **Pluggable** means: a subsystem can be replaced by a test double at a named
> call site, in this repository, and the compiler enforces that nothing else
> reaches around it.

Not compiled out, not dynamically loaded, not third-party. If someone later
wants "compiled out", that ticket adds the feature **and** the CI job that
builds without it, in the same commit.

### 8.3 The prerequisites, before a design ticket is written

1. **Step 2 lands.** A boundary cannot be drawn around a theme whose methods are
   interleaved with twenty-five others.
2. **A read/write/call audit of one bounded candidate**, chosen while Step 2 is
   in flight so §8 is not a standing start. It must cover `Deref`,
   destructuring, aliases, and callees that close over `&mut Runtime` — grepping
   for `WindowRuntime` misses all four.
3. **The contract written before the code**: immutable snapshots with stable
   `TabId`/`LeafId` and generations; typed outputs; exact synchronous
   application points; rejected and stale result handling; cancellation and
   drop; error propagation; redraw and deadline obligations; shared-resource
   borrowing.
4. **Evidence before the promise**: trace-based before/after event sequences, and
   a working no-op or replacement instance for the chosen seam.

The review's own suggestion for the first candidate is worth taking: **a
toast-only adapter** out of the attention theme is a smaller honest seam than
"attention & notifications", because it separates the ledger decision from the
delivery from the app mutation.

### 8.4 And `seats.rs` is Step 4's problem, not a separate plan's

The first draft deferred `seats.rs` (51,302 lines) to "its own plan" and gave a

The true and sufficient reason it is not in Steps 1–3: it is inside the

**the first thing Step 4 has to face**, because the document model it holds is
the hub §3.2 says no boundary may cross.

## 9. What NOT to do

1. **No new abstraction for its own sake.** Every trait must name the second
   implementation it exists for, and after finding 22 "a test double at a named
   call site" is the only second implementation this workspace can actually
   produce.
2. **No plugin API in this pass.** No dynamic loading, no stable ABI, no
   third-party surface, no manifest format. Rust has no stable ABI to hold such
   a boundary and nothing in the tree carries loader or registration machinery.
   The review examined this refusal and says keep it as worded.
3. **No behaviour change mixed into a move — enforced by a check that can
   actually see one.** *Rewritten.* The first draft's enforcement was a sorted
   multiset of source lines; it cannot detect a reordering of two effects, and
   it rejects the visibility, source-pin and platform-array edits that §6.1 and
   §6.2 show are mandatory parts of the same step. The rule stands; the
   mechanism is now §6.3's ordered parsed-token comparison with a **reviewed
   allow-list** naming exactly which non-relocation edits the ticket is
   permitted to carry. A rule the obeying ticket has to override is a rule that
   needed rewriting.
4. **No shared `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` tricks.** Two worktrees sharing one target
   directory has already cost this project a day. Every worktree keeps its own.
5. **No renaming during a move.** Method, test and module names survive Steps 2
   and 3 unchanged; a rename in the same commit destroys the only cheap
   verification either has.
6. **No `seats.rs` in the same pass as anything else** — for §8.4's reason, not
   the first draft's.
7. **No adding a `[lib]` to `bt-app` as a "quick win".** It saves no compile:
   with a `[lib]`, `cargo test` still codegens the crate body twice, as the rlib
   and as the `cfg(test)` harness, which is what it does today with the bin and
   the bin-harness. And each file under `tests/` is its own binary linking the
   whole rlib. *Two sentences corrected:* `crates/bt-app/tests/` **does** exist —
   the narrower true statement is that an integration test cannot reach a
   bin-only crate's items. And "a prerequisite for some later options" now names
   them: a second in-repo consumer of the app's types, and a public API against
   which §8's boundary could be checked by the compiler instead of by a source
   pin. **Neither is on this plan's schedule, which is the actual reason to
   refuse it now.**
8. **No promising the owner a compile-time number before Step 0 has run.**
9. **No planning Step 3 against any graph but `root_prod`.** Planning against
   the module-name graph is what produced a table with five impossible rows.

## 10. The gate-time model

**Current, as the owner reports it: 60–90 minutes for a full gate, 7–9 GB per
compile.** Nothing here measured that; it is taken as given.

*Measured facts the model is built on:*

- The gate is three commands (`CONTRIBUTING.md:11–19`) plus six scripts.
- `cargo test` codegens and links `bt-app` **twice** — the bin and the harness.
- `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets` runs under
  `RUSTC_WORKSPACE_WRAPPER`, so it has a different fingerprint from
  `cargo test`'s artifacts and reuses none of them.
- **`cargo clippy` is a check-mode command. It emits metadata and never links.**
  So `debug`, `opt-level` and the linker — all of Step 0's items — are codegen
  and link settings that reach **one** of the three gates, and inside it only
  the codegen-and-link half.
- The shortcut scripts force a full harness rebuild through a feature difference
  (§4.1), and run nowhere in CI.

*The model, per step, with each row naming which gate it moves:*

| Step | Gate it reaches | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 §4.1** the script selection fix | `cargo test`, and only when a shortcut script runs | removes one full `bt-app` codegen **and one 209.8 MiB link** from every gate run that includes the scripts. *Measurable today; free; independent of the split.* |
| **0 §4.3** `opt-level` overrides | `cargo test` | *estimated*: lets `cargo test` reuse what `cargo build` just built. On the owner's build-run-test loop this is plausibly the largest item in the document, and it is the reason it was promoted. |
| **0 §4.4** `debug = "line-tables-only"` | `cargo test`'s link | *estimated*: aimed at the **654.6 MiB PDB** and the link's debug-info work, **not** at the 209.8 MiB image |
| **0 §4.5** `rust-lld` | `cargo test`'s link | *estimated*, commonly large on MSVC links this size; trial only |
| **1** the `i18n` cut | none | **zero.** It buys Step 3. |
| **2** `main.rs` → `runtime/*.rs` | none | **zero to very slightly negative, and the plan says so.** It buys navigation and merges. |
| **3** eleven crates, 31,336 lines out | all three | *estimated* **6.9% off every rebuild** — half the first draft's claim — plausibly as little as 3.5%, because the extracted code is the simplest in the crate. Clippy and fmt benefit too, which no Step 0 item does. |
| **4** the orchestrator boundary | none | **zero.** Correctness and reviewability, not speed. |

**The honest summary for the owner: no step in this plan makes the gate fast, and
the one item that is free is in §4.1 and can land today.** Step 0's remaining
items reach one gate of three. Step 3 compounds across all three but at half the
advertised rate. Steps 1, 2 and 4 buy correctness, navigability and the ability
to run two tickets in parallel — a different currency, and at 112 of 150
arguably the more expensive one.

## 11. Open questions

The first draft's ten questions were answered by the review; the answers are in
its phase 5 and are folded into the sections above. What is still open, and what
the next review should attack:

1. **Is `bt-app-split-table.py`'s manifest right, or only self-consistent?** It
   proves every row's modules are free in the graph. It does **not** prove the
   grouping is the right one, or that a row's public API can be written without
   widening something. Each row's ticket still needs its own read.
2. **`APP_NAME` unlocks 11,129 lines and is a string constant.** Is that real,
   or does `version.rs`'s build-script `FOLIO_COMMIT` input make it a bigger
   move than it looks? This is the cheapest thing in §7.3 to check and the most
   valuable if it holds.
3. **Is a wake-callback adapter for `AppEvent` a Step 3 prerequisite or a Step 4
   design?** It unlocks five modules, and the review calls the watchers
   "lifecycle services, not pure leaf functions". If it is Step 4 work, §7.3's
   first row moves out of Step 3 entirely.
4. **Does `preview_viewport`'s inherent `impl Runtime` (`:994`) have an owner
   after Step 2?** It cannot move to another crate. Does it become a
   `runtime/preview.rs` method, and does that make the rest of the preview-text
   group extractable, or does it need the document types moved first anyway?
5. **Is `[profile.test] opt-level` the largest single item in the gate?** §4.3
   argues it plausibly is, on an artifact-sharing argument nobody has measured.
   If it is, the plan's whole emphasis is wrong and Step 0 is most of the win.
6. **Should `Text` be split by area?** Extracting `bt-i18n` is close to neutral
   on invalidation — `i18n.rs` is edited in 24 of the last 150 commits and every
   one of those recompiles the whole crate today. A by-area split could make
   most edits miss most consumers. It is a real option and it is its own plan.
7. **Is the freeze still the right protocol** once §6.2's three pieces land
   outside it? The review says yes and prices the alternative at 26–39 hours of
   gate time. That arithmetic should be re-checked against whatever Step 0
   actually does to the gate.
