## 0. The ruling this is written under, and the framing it revises twice

**The owner's direction, 2026-09-15: "modular, reusable, pluggable, with
well-designed interfaces; plan first; Codex reviews and improves the plan."**

The framing put to the owner before any measurement was: follow the seams the
workspace already has — `bt-term`, `bt-viewport`, `bt-render`, `bt-math`,
`bt-doc`, `bt-platform`, `bt-persist` are already separate crates — the disease
is the god object `Runtime` in `bt-app`, and the goal is a thin orchestrator over
subsystems behind narrow interfaces. Not a third-party plugin system.

**The shape of that framing survives. Its diagnosis was wrong twice, and each
correction cost something the plan had already promised.**

1. **`Runtime` is a two-field struct, but it is not a narrow facade.**
   `struct Runtime<'a> { app: &'a mut App, window: &'a mut WindowRuntime }`, and
   the crate pins that shape with a `const _` destructure. But it also
   implements `Deref<Target = TabState>` and `DerefMut` (`main.rs:16477`,
   `:16485`), so it hands out the active tab implicitly as well; and methods
   such as `answer_attention` (`:92786`) destructure `WindowRuntime` and mutate
   `tabs[index]` without ever writing `self.window.tabs`. The god objects are
   `App` (78 fields) and `WindowRuntime` (245 fields), 1,310 methods hang off
   `impl Runtime`, and **any conclusion about who owns what that was drawn from
   a field-name regex is a lower bound, not an ownership proof** (review
   finding 5). §3 keeps the census and withdraws the conclusions.

2. **The obstacle to extracting subsystems is the module cycle, and it is far
   worse than the first draft said.** Measured with items owned by `main.rs`
   resolved into a single `@root` node, the largest strongly connected component
   of `bt-app` is **80 nodes and 442,903 lines** — not the 43 nodes and 232,495
   lines the first draft reported, which counted only `crate::module::` edges
   and let a module that names a root-owned type look free. Both numbers are
   real measurements of different graphs; **`root_prod` is the one a crate split
   has to obey**, because a new crate cannot name a type that lives in the
   binary it was cut out of.

3. **So the extractable surface is 6.9% of the crate, not 14.5%.** After Step 1's
   cut, **22 modules and 31,358 lines** can leave with no root-owned item
   relocated first. The first draft claimed 51 modules and 66,273 lines. What
   survives intact is the *argument* for Step 1: the multiplier the `i18n` cut
   buys is 2.67× on the root-aware graph against 2.70× on the first one. What
   does not survive is the absolute mass, and everything §10 estimated from it.

**The plan therefore gains a step it did not have and loses a promise it should
not have made.** The step is Step 1, three functions in `i18n.rs`. The promise
is Step 3's size: sixteen crates become **thirteen rows over 22 modules**, and a
second table now names, per blocked module, the root-owned item that has to move
before it can follow.

## 1. The problem, in numbers

*Measured; inventory §§1–3. Where the review's independent count differs from
the first draft's, the review's is used and the inventory records both.*

- `bt-app` is **456,556 lines — 67.3% of all first-party Rust under `crates/`**
  (678,745). The first draft's 65.6% divided by a denominator that included
  `vendor/`.
- `crates/bt-app/src` is **102 files, 455,336 physical lines**. `main.rs` is
  **165,815** physical lines, of which **63,487 (38%) are two `impl Runtime<'_>`
  blocks** holding 1,310 methods. Five methods — `mouse_input`,
  `refresh_chrome`, `chrome_mouse_input`, `keyboard_input`, `create` — are 4,858
  lines between them.
- **43.3% of the crate is `#[cfg(test)]`** — 197,346 lines by the review's
  cfg-inheriting count; `main.rs` alone 59,903. **3,809 syntactic `#[test]`
  attributes**, 1,043 of them in `main.rs`, 770 of those in a *single* `mod
  tests` of 46,636 lines. None of these is a libtest census; a `#[test]` behind
  a platform `cfg` is counted here and may not run.
- `bt-app` has **no `[lib]` target**, so an integration test cannot reach its
  items and all its tests compile into **one** harness. (`crates/bt-app/tests/`
  does exist — it holds the `harness = false` macOS glyph target and a
  `fixtures/` directory. The constraint is "no library to link against", not
  "no `tests/` directory".)
- Artifact sizes from the owner's own earlier builds, **historical observations
  and not reproducible in this worktree**: product bin 85.8 MiB; unit-test
  harness 209.8 MiB with a **654.6 MiB PDB**; `target\debug` 74.5 GiB.
- `main.rs` grew **+34,764 lines in the fifteen days** from `v0.1.0-preview` to
  HEAD.
- **Of the 150 most recent `--no-merges` commits that touch `crates/bt-app`,
  ending at `76ca0788`, 112 also touch `main.rs` — 75%.** That is the whole of
  the claim: the unit is a non-merge commit already known to touch the crate,
  the file list is the one `git show --name-only` prints for it, and the window
  is those 150 commits and no other. The same pass gives `seats.rs` 29,
  `i18n.rs` 24, `preview.rs` 17, `settings.rs` 12. The exact command and all
  five readings of the same window are in inventory §2.3.

  **What it does not imply** (R2-12). It is not a statement about lines, about
  merges, or about any commit outside the window; and it is **not a probability
  for the branches that happen to be open on the day Step 2 is dispatched** —
  §6.5 looks at those instead of inferring them. The first draft's "98 of 150"
  is not unreproducible, as revision 2 claimed: it comes out of enumerating
  `git log -150 --format=%H` under **default** history simplification and then
  running `git show --name-only` on each hash. That sentence is withdrawn; what
  a churn number needs stated is its revision range, its traversal **and** its
  file-display procedure, because those three choices span 45 to 119 over one
  window.

The consequences the owner feels — a 60–90 minute gate, batches that conflict,
agents navigating a god object — are downstream of those six lines.

**One number the first draft used is withdrawn entirely.** It said 58% of the
crate is code and "the remaining 42% is prose", and divided estimates by 1.7×
accordingly. The complement of the 263,947 code lines includes blank lines and
string literals as well as comments, so neither the description nor the factor
is sound. No estimate in this revision uses it.

## 3. The state census, and what may and may not be concluded from it

The first draft called this "the interface map" and drew ownership conclusions
from it. Finding 5 disproves those conclusions. **What is kept is the census;
what is withdrawn is every sentence that turned a field count into a boundary.**

### 3.1 The census

*Measured, lexically.* For every one of the 1,310 methods, which
`self.window.<field>` and `self.app.<field>` names appear in its body:

- `WindowRuntime` has 245 fields; **242** are reached by direct access allowing
  whitespace (239 by contiguous match). `App` has 78; **78** are reached.
- Of the 239 contiguously-reached `WindowRuntime` fields, **86 are touched by
  exactly one theme** and **38 by five or more**.
- The worst: `renderer` (220 methods / 25 themes), `tabs` (233 / 23 —
  **255 allowing whitespace**), `active_tab` (154 / 21), `window` (79 / 21),
  `app.motion` (90 / 18), `app.settings_store` (84 / 20), `app.gpu` (64 / 21).

### 3.2 What that is evidence of, and what it is not

**It is evidence of** one thing and one only: where state is concentrated.
Three fields — `tabs`, `active_tab`, `window` — are named in the bodies of most
of the crate. That much is safe.

Revision 2 added that the service objects behind them "are read far more often
than written". **That is withdrawn** (R2-12): the census cannot see reads apart
from writes, which the paragraph below says in its own third bullet, so the
sentence claimed exactly what the instrument cannot measure. Restoring it needs
a read/write/callee audit, which is §8.3's prerequisite 2 and not a by-product
of a field-name count.

**It is not evidence of ownership, and not of a one-way dependency**, for three
reasons the review names:

- `Runtime: Deref<Target = TabState>`, so any method may reach the active tab
  with no `self.window.` prefix at all. A regex counting field access cannot see
  it.
- `answer_attention` (`main.rs:92786`) destructures `WindowRuntime` and mutates
  `tabs[index]`. The census records that method as not touching `tabs`.
- A field *name* appearing in a body says nothing about read versus write, and
  nothing about what the method's callees do with the `&mut Runtime` they were
  handed.

**So the first draft's table of "genuinely one-way subsystems" is withdrawn.**
Seven themes were listed as already one-way; the review's finding 14 shows
`attention`, `search`, `first run`, `quake`, `math`, `palette` and `diagnostics`
each mutating state the table said they did not touch. §8 no longer promises
them.

**What replaces it** is a smaller, defensible statement, and it is what Step 4
starts from rather than what Step 4 concludes:

> The three hub fields — `tabs`, `active_tab`, `window` — are the document
> model. No boundary that leaves them on the far side of an interface will hold,
> and no interface that hands a subsystem `&mut tabs` is a boundary. Everything
> else is an open question until a read/write/call audit answers it.

**What each flag does on the installed toolchain, checked against that
toolchain rather than remembered** (R4-1). Cargo is **1.94.1**, the version
`rust-toolchain.toml` pins, and the manual shipped beside it is the citation.

- **`--timings` writes HTML, and only HTML.** That version's own manual page
  (`share/man/man1/cargo-build.1`, and the same paragraph in `cargo-test.1`)
  says a file `cargo-timing.html` is written to the **`target/cargo-timings`**
  directory at the end of the build, that an additional report **with a
  timestamp in its filename** is written beside it, and that these reports
  "are suitable for human consumption only, and **do not provide
  machine-readable timing data**". The timestamp is UTC, `YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ`.
- **So there is no machine-readable timing output on this channel.** The `json`
  form — `--timings=json` — is an unstable option behind `-Z unstable-options`
  on nightly; the stable flag is the bare `--timings`, which means `html`. **This
  plan does not move to nightly**, so the HTML report is the whole of the timing
  evidence and the numbers below are read out of it by hand.
- **`--message-format=json` is a different flag and it is stable.** It is not a
  form of `--timings`, it does not imply `--timings`, and `--timings` does not
  imply it: it changes the **diagnostic output format** and puts Cargo's machine
  messages on **stdout**, one JSON object per line. The census rows below need
  it and the timing row needs `--timings`, so **each of the three preparation
  commands carries both**. Revision 4 put the JSON flag on one command of three,
  which left `build` and `clippy` with no artefact census at all. On clippy it
  goes **before the `--`**, because everything after `--` belongs to
  `clippy-driver`; the redirections are the shell's and may sit anywhere.
- **`-v` writes to stderr**, which is why the two streams are captured apart
  rather than merged: the JSON is the census, and a verbose line inside the
  `.jsonl` file makes it unparseable.
- **Read a record's kind before its fields.** Every line carries a `reason`.
  `"reason":"compiler-artifact"` lines are the artefact census;
  **`"reason":"build-script-executed"` lines are build-script executions and are
  a separate population** — never counted as compilations and never summed with
  them (R4-1). `compiler-message` and `build-finished` lines are neither.

**Two checks on the capture itself, because a shell can destroy it silently.**
Windows PowerShell 5.1 writes redirected output as **UTF-16LE** and turns a
native command's stderr into error records; the artefact stream survives
neither. So record the shell and `$PSVersionTable.PSVersion` in the run record,
and after the first command of the first repetition check two properties once:
the `.jsonl` file begins with the bytes `{"`, has no BOM and no interleaved NUL,
and **every one of its lines parses as JSON**; and the `-verbose.log` beside it
contains **no** JSON line. If either fails, capture from PowerShell 7 or
`cmd.exe` instead and say in the record which shell produced the evidence.

**Archive after every command, not after the run** (R4-1). The HTML lands
**inside** the target directory, which the cold case deletes in full before each
repetition, and `cargo-timing.html` is overwritten by the next `--timings`
command in the same repetition. So, immediately after each of the three
preparation commands and **before the next one runs**:

1. **Copy the newest `cargo-timing-*.html`** out of `<target>\cargo-timings\`
   into `$ev` as `<n>-<command>-timing.html`, and record **its original
   timestamped filename** beside the copy — that name, not the copy's, is the
   report's identity. Do not archive `cargo-timing.html`: it is a duplicate of
   whichever report ran last, and it is the file that proves nothing about which
   command produced it.
2. **Record the image sizes that command produced**, each as **path, size in
   bytes and mtime**: after command 1, `target\debug\folio.exe` and
   `target\debug\folio.pdb`; after command 2, those two paths **again** — cargo
   uplifts the test-profile bin over them (§4.4b) — plus the harness executable,
   whose path is read from that command's own `compiler-artifact` record for
   `bt-app` with `profile.test` true, through its `filenames`.
3. **Append one row to the run record**: the command as typed, the working
   directory, the exit status, the UTC start and end timestamps, and the elapsed
   difference between them.

**Time each command with timestamps taken around it** —
`(Get-Date).ToUniversalTime().ToString("o")` before and after, subtracted — and
**do not use `Measure-Command`**: it consumes the output stream the JSON capture
needs, which is how the first draft of this plan proposed to measure the wrong
thing in the first place.

**Record the command context explicitly; do not reconstruct it from the log**
(R4-1). A `-v` log shows the commands that actually ran, so it says nothing
about the effective settings of a unit that did not run, and a reused unit is
exactly the case this baseline is about. Independently of the log, once per
repetition, capture into `$ev`: `cargo --version --verbose` and `rustc -vV`;
`rustup show active-toolchain`; the repository's `.cargo\config.toml` **and**
the machine's `%USERPROFILE%\.cargo\config.toml`, which carries this project's
standing `jobs` ruling and is therefore part of the measurement; the value of
`RUSTFLAGS`, `CARGO_BUILD_JOBS`, `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, `CARGO_INCREMENTAL` and
every `CARGO_PROFILE_*` variable, each recorded as **set-to** or **unset**;
`git rev-parse HEAD` and `git status --porcelain` for the source state; and the
resolved target triple. The **feature** evidence is not taken from the log
either — it is the `features` field of the `compiler-artifact` records, which is
per target rather than per package.

**Ask stable Cargo only for what stable Cargo emits** (R2-9), **and only for
boundaries it exposes** (R3-1). Revision 2 asked for "`--timings` JSON" and said
the HTML "names the link step alone"; revision 3 replaced that with the HTML but
kept reading link time off "the tail of the binary's own unit" and treating
`-v`'s `Fresh` lines as a complete artefact census. **Both of those are
withdrawn.** Cargo's HTML report has no observable codegen-start boundary inside
a binary unit, so a "tail" has no edge to be measured from; and the installed
Cargo emits `Fresh` **per package**, conditionally and deduplicated — one
package can have a reused normal bin and a rebuilt test harness and print one
line for both. `-Z` options are not available on the pinned stable toolchain and
the plan does not move to nightly to make them so.

**The evidence schema. Each row names the command, the artefact, and the field
read from it — and nothing in it is inferred from a boundary Cargo does not
draw.**

| Quantity | Command that produces it | Artefact, and the exact field read |
| --- | --- | --- |

**Do not treat the JSON as a serialized Cargo unit graph.** Compiler-artifact
records describe the targets that were built or reused; they are not Cargo's

**The three repetitions, spelled out so that three invocations are not silently
one cold run and two warm ones** (R3-2), **and so that every repetition of a
case starts from the same build state as the one before it** (R4-2):

- **Cold** — before *each* of the three repetitions, delete the **whole** target
  directory named in the ticket, then run the **full** four-command sequence.
  Resetting only the first command would leave repetitions two and three warm
  while all three are labelled cold. **What is not deleted is the package
  cache**: `%USERPROFILE%\.cargo\registry` and `\git` stay, so "cold" means *no
  compiled artefacts*, not *no downloads*, and the figure does not contain
  network time. Record whether the commands ran `--offline`, and hold that
  constant across all three cases.
- **Warm, no edit** — establish the warm cache once by running the full sequence
  to green **from a deleted target directory**, and discard that run's records;
  then run the sequence three times with nothing touched between them.
  "Nothing touched" is a checkable claim, not an intention: no file in the
  worktree is written — `git status --porcelain` is empty before and after, and
  no source file's mtime moves — no branch or toolchain is switched, no second
  Cargo process runs against that target directory, and the environment of the
  three runs is the one recorded above. Because the priming run is the same four
  commands, each of the three repetitions starts from an identical, fully built
  cache.
- **App-only edit** — each measured repetition runs from the **unedited warm
  state**, never from the state its predecessor left behind. One repetition is
  therefore four steps: **(1)** a discarded **re-priming** run of the full
  sequence on the *unedited* source, to green — this is what puts the cache
  back, because **restoring the file's bytes does not undo the compilation the
  edited build performed**, and without it repetitions two and three measure a
  rebuild of an already-edited crate; **(2)** apply the literal edit below;
  **(3)** run the measured sequence; **(4)** restore the file byte-for-byte.
  Repetition one may take the warm case's priming run as its re-priming run if
  nothing has been built in between; repetitions two and three may not.

**The cache regime is named, and it is this one: source restore plus a discarded
re-priming run.** No snapshot of the target directory is taken. A ticket that
prefers a snapshot restore instead must restore the **whole** target tree with
mtimes preserved and **prove the restore worked** — a `cargo build --workspace
--locked` on the restored tree that compiles **nothing** — and say so in
writing. An unverified directory copy is not a restored cache: Cargo's freshness
decision reads mtimes, and a copy that moves them turns a warm cache into an
expensive one. **Every re-priming run is discarded, labelled as a re-priming run
in the record, and never counted as a repetition.**

Record the target triple, the toolchain, the effective flags, `jobs`, the target
directory and the resolved feature set — the context list above is what collects
them — and **hold all six constant across the three cases**, along with
`CARGO_INCREMENTAL`, the declared source state and the command order.

#### The app-only edit, literally

*§4.0's sixth box requires this, and revisions 3 and 4 told the ticket writer to
supply it rather than supplying it (R4-2). Here it is, as bytes.*

**It is a production-code edit inside one function body, and not a comment.**
Which it is has to be said, because the two measure different things: the dev
profile is incremental and `[profile.test]` inherits that, so a comment-only
change lets the incremental cache skip most of the crate's codegen — a real
number, and the cheapest one available. A statement inside a function body
invalidates that function's work and everything downstream of it inside the
crate, and forces the link. **What it still is not** is an edit to a widely
referenced item or a public signature, which invalidates more of the incremental
cache; this case is the common one — one developer, one function — and the
record says so rather than generalising from it.

- **File** — `crates/bt-app/src/main.rs`.
- **Anchor** — the single line `fn main() -> Result<()> {`. At `ee771130` it is
  line **116069**. At the release base, **find it by its text and assert it
  matches exactly once** (`Select-String -SimpleMatch "fn main() -> Result<()> {"`
  returns one line), then record the line number the run actually used. The
  anchor is unique today: the file's other `fn main` spellings are the test
  literals `fn main() {}`, which do not match it.
- **The insertion** — one new line **immediately after** the anchor line, with
  four leading spaces and terminated by a single `\n`:

  ```rust
      let _baseline_rep: u8 = std::hint::black_box(0);
  ```

  `std::hint::black_box` is already used in this file (`main.rs:151289` at
  `ee771130`), so the idiom compiles here and there is no import to add; the
  underscore-prefixed binding draws no `unused` lint under the gate's
  `-D warnings`; and the annotated type makes the call monomorphic, so the
  statement is really codegen'd rather than folded away.
- **The removal** — delete that one line. Nothing else in the file is touched at
  any point, and the file is **LF** (`.gitattributes`): an editor that rewrites
  it as CRLF fails the hash check below, and takes the source pins with it.
- **The assertions**, in this order. *Before the edit:*
  `git status --porcelain crates/bt-app/src/main.rs` is empty, and
  `git hash-object crates/bt-app/src/main.rs` and the file's line count are
  recorded. *After the edit:* `git diff --numstat -- crates/bt-app/src/main.rs`
  reports **one line added, zero removed, for that one path**, and the line count
  is exactly one greater — that is the replacement-count assertion, and it is
  what catches an editor that reformatted something else on the way past.
  *After the restore:* `git restore --source=HEAD -- crates/bt-app/src/main.rs`,
  then `git status --porcelain` empty again **and** `git hash-object` equal to
  the recorded hash. A restore that does not reproduce the hash is a failed
  repetition, not a rounding error.
- **The edit must leave the suite green, and the sequence's fourth command is
  what proves it.** `main.rs` is read as text by 91 `include_str!` pins
  (§6.2b): three of them forbid named spellings over the whole source and ten
  count occurrences of a needle. The probe line is chosen to be inert against
  all of them — it contains none of the withdrawn spellings and adds no
  occurrence of any counted needle — but **the guarantee is the run, not the
  argument**: if a measured repetition goes red, the probe is wrong, the
  repetition is discarded, and the fix is a different inert line. **Never a
  changed pin.**

**The trials are not additive** (R2-9). §4.3, §4.4b and §4.5 are each measured
alone, and then **the chosen combination is measured again**: changing test
debuginfo after aligning `opt-level` can split units that the alignment had just
made shared. The number the owner is given is the combined measurement, never
the sum of three separate wins.

what the alignment can and cannot remove.*

`[profile.test] opt-level = 1` is at `Cargo.toml:193–194`. **There is no
`[profile.dev]` in this workspace** — the only `^[profile` lines are 193 and 199
— so dev is the built-in default at `opt-level = 0`. A profile applies to every
target and reaches every dependency, so `cargo test` compiles the graph at `1`
while `cargo build` and `cargo run` compile the same graph at `0`, and the
owner's loop is build, run, test on one machine.

The measurement is therefore not "is the suite slower at 0". It is *what does
the gate cost when `cargo test` can reuse what `cargo run` just built*: three
runs of `cargo test --workspace --locked --no-run` immediately after a green
`cargo build --workspace --locked`, at `1` and at `0`.

If the suite does need optimised dependencies, the setting that keeps both
properties is the override rather than the blanket key:

```toml
[profile.dev.package."*"]
opt-level = 1

[profile.test.package."*"]
opt-level = 1
```

**What that override does and does not reach** ([Cargo profile
overrides](https://doc.rust-lang.org/cargo/reference/profiles.html#overrides)).
`package."*"` matches dependencies and **excludes workspace members** — so
`bt-app`, `bt-math`, `bt-render`, `bt-term` and the vendored
`alacritty_terminal`, which is a workspace member here, all stay at `0` under
both commands. That is the intent, and it is *not* "all of the app's
dependencies are optimised": build dependencies and proc macros are governed
separately, and generic code from a dependency that is instantiated in the app
is codegen'd with the app's settings, not the dependency's
([generics](https://doc.rust-lang.org/cargo/reference/profiles.html#overrides-and-generics)).

**Three claims revision 2 made here are withdrawn** (R2-6):

- *"The two share nothing."* Too strong as an inference and unnecessary as a
  claim: what is true is that a differing profile setting changes a unit's
  fingerprint, so units compiled under it are not reused. Reuse is conditional
  on matching target, mode, features, profile and flags, and the trial's job is
  to **record which units actually become fresh**, not to assert a total.
- *"The flags match unit for unit."* They match for the units the override
  reaches. Say that instead.
- *Whether `[profile.test]` inherits `[profile.dev]`'s override table is "the
  one thing the trial must settle".* It is documented that `test` inherits
  `dev`; what the trial records is the **effective** flags from `cargo test -v`,
  which is worth doing anyway and is not an open semantic question.

**And alignment does not delete the app's test harness.** `cargo test` still
builds a `--test`/`cfg(test)` unit of `bt-app` that is a different unit from the
normal `folio` bin; aligning `opt-level` lets the *dependencies* and the normal
bin be reused, and leaves the harness compiling the crate's own test code and
linking a large executable. Price that harness's codegen, its link and the
suite's runtime at app `opt-level = 0` as part of the trial. **Nothing here
promises the historically described 3,749-test harness goes away**; no harness
census or timing was taken for this plan, and alignment does not split it.

**The risk this item carries is a clock, not a correctness failure.** The
robustness child at `main.rs:116322` runs under **five distinct limits** — a
60-second outer watchdog (`:116340`), a 30-second font warm-up (`:116384`) and
**three** request budgets, 5 s and 1 s at `:116402`–`:116404` and 10 s at
`:116410` — plus a 1-second completion `recv_timeout` at `:116427`. An
unoptimised Typst conversion can trip any of them without changing a single
functional output. Record the measured margin against **each** of those limits,
on the Windows CI runner as well as on the owner's machine.

**The contained-child protocol, written once for the two rows that use it.**
Both run the **same test identity** in a child copy of the harness, and the
parent switches nothing.

1. **The parent never installs a language.** Its whole body is spawn, wait,
   check — which is what keeps the 3,809 other tests out of it.
2. It launches `std::env::current_exe()` with the **full test identity** and
   `--exact` — for example

   "--nocapture"]` — so that exactly one test is selected.
3. It sets a **child marker** environment variable whose value is the path of a
   completion-marker file. With the marker set the test body takes the child
   branch and does the language work; without it, the body is the parent branch.
   **That is what stops the child spawning a grandchild.**
4. It requires the child to **exit successfully** *and* the completion marker to
   exist, written **after the child's last assertion**. Success alone is not
   acceptance: **a libtest run that matches no test exits 0** (§6.2c), so a
   renamed identity would otherwise leave a green parent testing nothing.
5. It **bounds** the child with a named timeout, kills it on expiry and fails.
6. The child installs one language, asserts every case for it, installs the
   other, asserts again, and only then writes the marker. It may install
   languages freely: it is a process of its own, and `CURRENT` in it is nobody
   else's.

## 6. Step 2 — `main.rs` into `runtime/*.rs`, by theme

**And it is not dispatchable as a relocation ticket, on this revision's own

guard preparation, on the topology that exists. The move itself waits on an
item-level destination and visibility map, §6.2(b)'s class-specific pin

### 6.1 What moves, and the scope this plan picks

Rust allows `impl Runtime<'_>` blocks in as many files of one crate as you like,
and an item declared private at the crate root stays visible in every descendant
module — so `runtime::preview` can `use crate::{Runtime, WindowRuntime,
TabState};` and touch private fields exactly as `main.rs` does today.

**But private *inherent methods* do not work that way** (finding 9). A private
method moved into one child module is not reachable from its parent or from a
sibling. `open_search` (`main.rs:42046`) calls `refresh_chrome` (`:39271`) and
the two land in different themes; there are many such pairs. **The ticket
therefore explicitly permits and reviews the minimum `pub(crate)` or
`pub(in crate::runtime)` on every method called outside its new module**, keeps
narrow helper visibility everywhere else, and lists the widened set for review.
Note which of the two it needs: a caller still at the crate root needs
`pub(crate)`, and `pub(in crate::runtime)` does not reach it. `use super::*` in a
sibling test module does not restore access to a sibling's private definitions.

**The scope, decided here rather than left open** (R2-1 asks for this in
writing). **Step 2a moves the two `impl Runtime<'_>` blocks and nothing else** —
`main.rs:36598–61746` (25,149 lines) and `:62188–100525` (38,338), 63,487 lines
and 1,310 methods between them. Free functions, `static` and `const`
declarations, trait impls — `impl Deref for Runtime` at `:16477` is one, outside
both blocks — and the inline test modules **stay where they are in 2a**. Three
reasons, and the third is the one that decides it:

1. It is the only scope whose residue is a measured number. **165,815 − 63,487 =
   102,328** lines stay in `main.rs` after 2a, before any module or import lines
   are added. (Revision 2 said 102,329, keeping a newline-plus-one minuend from
   an earlier count; R2-12 is right. The first draft's "roughly 40,000" is a
   possible *post-2b* scale and was never a post-2a one.)
2. Every added scope is a different residue, a different file list and a
   different set of guards to edit, and none of those has been measured.
3. **It moves no platform `cfg` at all**, which takes the whole of revision 2's
   §6.2(a) out of 2a. Measured at this snapshot, the guard's own lexical rule
   matches **eleven** lines of `main.rs` — **ten compiler `cfg` attributes, plus
   one line of test code that merely contains the spellings as string literals**
   (R3-6; revision 3 called all eleven platform attributes). Every one of them
   is outside both impl blocks: `:112433` and `:112442` in
   `set_option_as_alt`; `:114252`,
   `:114261`, `:114272` and `:114293` around `window_surface_target`; `:114545`
   and `:114547` in `native_window`; `:116199` and `:116223` in **`fn main`**;
   and one test needle at `:163322`. **A strict two-impl 2a moves none of
   them.**

Proposed shape, unchanged in spirit: `runtime/mod.rs` plus about twenty-six
theme files, the largest ~9,000 lines (preview) and most under 2,500. **The
theme list is not the file list.** Twenty-six wildcard destinations are not a
dispatch manifest: the ticket carries a relocation manifest naming, item by
item, the destination file, the visibility it needs and the callers that change
with it.

### 6.2 The work that is not relocation, and when each piece can land

*R2-1, which is blocking. Revision 2 said all three non-relocation pieces should
land as reviewed commits **before** the freeze, so that the frozen window
contained "text relocation only". **Two of the three cannot land before the
move**, because each of them describes a topology that does not exist yet:*

- A new `runtime/launch.rs` entry in `FILES_THAT_MAY_NAME_A_PLATFORM`, added
  before anything moves, names a file that does not yet name a platform. The
  Rust guard's `silent` assertion (`main.rs:163122`) fails on it, and
  `check-portable-core.ps1`'s `$silent` throw fails on it too. **Both gates go
  red on the preparation commit.**
- A re-pointed `include_str!` naming a file that does not exist is a compile
  error; one naming a file that exists but does not yet hold the subject makes
  `body(signature)` panic with `"{signature} is declared in this file"`.
- A child selector aimed at a future test identity finds **zero** tests, and a
  zero-match libtest run **exits 0** — so it passes silently until the
  completion marker below exists to make it loud.

And the third claim in revision 2 — that after those preparations the freeze
window contains "text relocation only" — is false regardless: moving private
methods needs the visibility, import and path edits §6.1 lists.

**So the rule is a division, not an ordering.**

| Lands **before** the freeze, on the current topology | Lands **atomically with the move it belongs to** |
| --- | --- |
| recursive enumeration in every guard that walks the source tree | every `FILES_THAT_MAY_NAME_A_PLATFORM` entry, removal and arity change |
| a precise pin **subject map** — each `include_str!` invocation, its binding, its subjects, the scope each assertion covers, and the file each subject is going to | every `include_str!` path change |
| **class-specific preparation, per §6.2(b)'s four-row table**: unique subject and loud absence for the named-body and scoped guards, **complete source enumeration** for the whole-source negatives, and preserved scope and count ownership for the arity sites | every exact-path test selector |
| the child's **completion proof, under its current identity** | every count/arity assertion whose count the move changes |
| — | **every visibility and import change**, narrowly enumerated and reviewed as part of the move it serves |

**Not "a missing-subject failure on every pin"** (R4-4). Revision 4 wrote that
rule into this row and into §6.5's freeze protocol, and it is the rule R3-4
already rejected for half the classes. **A whole-source prohibition has no
subject to remove** — it is green precisely because its forbidden text is
absent — so what it needs prepared is forbidden-text injection into each covered
destination class, plus proof that its enumeration really reads every source
file it is supposed to cover. **An arity assertion owns a scope and a count**,
and a preparation that narrows either of them silently is the defect, not the
guard. And for any negative, **a missing input source and an absent forbidden
spelling are different facts**: a guard that reads nothing must fail loudly
rather than pass. §6.2(b)'s table is the authority for every class, and a
preparation ticket copies that table rather than this row.

**The visibility row moved columns in this revision** (R3-7). Revision 3 listed
it on the left, which contradicted both §6.1 — where the `pub(crate)` a method
needs is decided by where its *caller* ends up — and §6.5's own rule that each
move carries its own visibility and import edits. A visibility widening applied
before its method moves is a widening with no reason on the tree it lands on,
and a reviewer cannot check it against anything. **Only demonstrably
topology-compatible work belongs in the left column**, and the test for that is
the one `the_shell_page_is_gone` passes below: it changes nothing today and is
correct afterwards.

**Both failure directions must be demonstrated during implementation**, not
asserted: plant a platform `cfg` in a new `runtime/*.rs` file and watch the Rust
twin and the PowerShell script both go red (a stranger); take the last platform
`cfg` out of a listed file and watch them go red again (a silent name). That is
`CONTRIBUTING.md`'s "a new guard proves it fires", applied in both directions.

**Review the commits separately; merge them as one validated batch.** A commit
that is green on its own base is not green on the tree it is merged into, which
is §6.5's whole subject.

| Class | Where | What it actually is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| whole-`SOURCE` negative | `main.rs:15772`, `:105705` | `!SOURCE.contains(…)` over the whole file. These two **keep** their full-source coverage; a fragment-scoped replacement would weaken them. |

**`main.rs:103570` stays scoped.** Converting it to a whole-crate prohibition
would reject the current program — those five names legitimately occur elsewhere

leaving it alone. What it gains instead is a **required unique subject and a
loud failure when that subject is absent**: `body("    fn mini_source")` must
find exactly one `mini_source` header, in whichever file that method ends up in,
and must fail rather than return an empty fragment.

**So "88 loud plus three global" is withdrawn as a proven partition, and no
partition replaces it here.** The honest statement is the one R2-2 asks for:
**the 91 invocations have to be reclassified by subject during implementation.**
The method, which is the pin subject map of §6.2's first column:

1. For each invocation, record its binding name, its **subjects** (the
   signatures, needles and counts it actually reads) and the **scope** each
   assertion covers — the whole source, a named body, or a count over the whole
   source.
2. Record the destination file of each subject. **A shared `SOURCE` can host
   several subjects bound for different files**, so "one include replacement per
   invocation" is not generally sufficient; some invocations become a
   `const SOURCES: &[&str]` slice, and some split. **That slice is a shape to be
   designed, not one already in the tree** (R3-4): `main.rs:165696–165697`
   declares **two separate constants**, `SOURCE` and `PREVIEW_EDIT`, and
   `const SOURCES` occurs nowhere in `main.rs`. It is precedent for one test
   module reading two files, and nothing more.
3. **Mutation-check every converted guard, by class.** Revision 3 wrote one
   rule — remove the subject and watch it fail — and R3-4 shows that rule is
   wrong for two of the four classes. **A negative assertion is green precisely
   because its forbidden text is absent**, so removing the subject changes
   nothing: `main.rs:113789` would pass a mutation that was supposed to break
   it. And an arity assertion narrowed to one destination file can keep its
   expected count and still stop seeing an unauthorised caller added in another
   fragment of the former `main.rs`. So:

   | Guard class | The mutation evidence that class requires |
   | --- | --- |
   | **named body / positive requirement** (the `body(signature)` majority) | Remove **or rename** the unique subject and require failure; separately remove the required condition **inside the correct subject** and require failure. Reject a missing subject, reject a duplicated subject, and reject a match that landed on a test literal rather than the real declaration. |
   | **whole-source prohibition** (`:15772`, `:105705`, `:113789`) | **Inject one forbidden spelling into each covered destination class** and require failure; remove it and require recovery. Then, separately, **prove the enumeration is complete** — that every source file the guard is supposed to cover is really in the set it reads. A negative that reads four files out of five is green for the wrong reason. |
   | **arity / uniqueness** (the ten count sites) | **Delete a required occurrence** and require failure; **and add an unauthorised occurrence in a different covered fragment** and require failure. Record the expected production and test occurrences **separately, each with its owner**, so the count can be re-derived rather than guessed. |
   | **scoped negative** (`:103570`) | Both of the named-body rules, plus: inject a forbidden name **inside** `mini_source`'s body and require failure, and inject the same name **outside** it and require the guard stays green. That asymmetry is the guard's whole meaning. |

4. **Define the covered union, and do not let it drift.** It is the original
   `main.rs` scope **and its relocated descendants** — the files Step 2 creates
   out of it. Extending a whole-source guard to unrelated existing modules is a
   semantic decision about what the program forbids and needs an explicit
   ruling; it is not a side effect of a file move.
5. For any assertion converted to a whole-crate scan, define **test-literal and
   comment handling**, because a whole-crate scan matches its own text. The
   crate already knows this — `:15767`, `:105701` and `:113781` assemble their
   needles with `concat!` or `format!` for exactly that reason — and a converted
   guard has to keep the discipline.
6. **Preserve whole-source coverage across 2a and 2b, or replace it in writing.**
   A negative that covered all of `main.rs` before the split must still cover all
   of `main.rs` **plus** `runtime/*.rs` after it — or be replaced by named
   production and test invariants of equal intended strength, each stated.
   Silently narrowing a prohibition to whichever file the subject ended up in is
   the failure mode this rule exists for.
7. **Arity assertions belong to the move that changes their count, and the
   change is derived, not lowered.** `main.rs:105784` expects
   `SOURCE.matches("commit_leaf_resize(").count() == 6`, and its own comment at

   **four test callers**. When 2b moves those test callers out of `main.rs`, six
   is wrong — and the fix is **not** "lower it to the number left in this file".
   The ticket either keeps the union (count across the moved set) or splits the
   assertion into a production invariant and a test invariant with the owners
   written down. In the same commit, not before it and not after it.

### 6.3 How it is verified, and what the verification does and does not prove

*Finding 11, and **R2-11**, which is about the sentence "zero behaviour change"
rather than about the checks themselves.*

**What the ticket compares:**

- **A one-to-one map of old to new test identity**, carrying target, full
  qualified name, `cfg`, `#[ignore]` status and **multiplicity**. Leaf-name sets
  do not prove preserved coverage, and `--list` reports identities for **one
  built configuration** on one platform. Where a whole test module can keep its
  old logical path, `#[path]` preserves it and should.
- **Each relocated item paired old-to-new by fully qualified identity**, with
  kind, span, signature, attributes and `cfg`, impl or trait context, and an
  **ordered, literal-preserving token body**. Missing and duplicated pairings are
  themselves failures. The sorted-multiset line diff revision 1 proposed is gone:
  sorting loses control-flow order, so swapping two effects passes it, and it
  *rejects* the visibility, pin and platform edits §6.1 and §6.2 show are
  mandatory.
- **A list, not a regex exemption.** Every permitted change is named with its old
  and new tokens and its reason. A blanket exemption for "paths and visibility"
  is the same mistake in a smaller box — and an extractor that blanks string
  literals the way the dependency graph does would also erase literal changes
  Step 1 forbids, so the comparison keeps literals.
- **Bindings, macro inputs and file-relative inputs audited separately**, because
  a token body can be identical while a bare helper resolves to a different item
  and a `file!()`-relative input moves under it.
- **rustfmt run over the new tree and reviewed separately**, against the same
  semantic pairs; indentation and import position change its output and that must
  not arrive mixed into the relocation diff.
- **What can change without changing a token**: `cfg`-scoped imports, inherited
  lint attributes, dead-code exposure, and clippy on both supported app
  platforms. CI reality: macOS checks `bt-app` with `--all-targets`
  (`ci.yml:517`); **Linux explicitly excludes `bt-app`** (`:644`, portable list
  at `:649`). The existing Linux job does not validate an app split.
- **The full behavioural gate per landing**: the three gates, plus
  `check-portable-core.ps1`, `check-shortcuts-table.ps1`,
  `check-ignored-tests.ps1` and the other applicable repository scripts.

**And now the part revision 2 left implicit.** Ordered bodies plus a full green
gate **do not prove zero behaviour change**, and this plan does not say they do.

*What they do support:* an identical test set and identical outcomes; an
identical ignored list; identical `FILES_THAT_MAY_NAME_A_PLATFORM` results in
both directions; each moved item's body executing its effects in the same order;
and no changed public string or `Text` variant.

*What they cannot reach:* timing, native IME/TSF input, window focus, rendering
and platform behaviour, none of which unit success observes; and Linux's
exclusion of `bt-app` means one of the three CI platforms contributes nothing
here.

*And what is not available as evidence, so that nobody proposes it as the
missing proof:* **a per-item `cargo rustc --emit=llvm-ir` or `--emit=mir`
comparison is not feasible across a file move.** Module paths are part of symbol
names and of `DefPath`, so the IR for a moved item differs by construction, and
the crate has one bin target rather than per-item units to emit. For the same
reason **a byte-identical release binary is not expected and is not a goal**: a
moved item's symbol path changes. Saying otherwise would be inventing a check
nobody can run.

**So the claim this step makes, in the words it may be made in:** *no intended
product behaviour change, supported by the comparisons and tests above*, with
the changed test, source and diagnostic paths identified. Not a formal
equivalence proof.

### 6.4 Expected effect

**Compile time: zero to very slightly negative, and the plan says so.** Type
checking, name resolution and macro expansion are per-crate; incremental
compilation is keyed on items and queries, not files; and CI sets
`CARGO_INCREMENTAL: 0`. Twenty-six modules add a little name-resolution and
metadata work. The review independently confirms this reading.

What it buys: **navigation** (the handoff already instructs agents not to read
the file but to grep it, because it cannot be read) and **merges** — at 112 of
150 commits, a preview ticket and a git-panel ticket currently land in the same
file and after the split they do not. It is also the precondition for §8.

**Struck:** the first draft offered "Step 2 may fix `cargo test --release`" as a
benefit. `cargo test --release` selects `[profile.release]` with `lto = "fat"`
and `codegen-units = 1`; neither depends on file count, and rustc's stack
exhaustion is per-item recursion, which splitting a file does not make shallower
for any single item (finding 23). Two cheaper candidates should be tried first
and on their own: **`RUST_MIN_STACK` raised for the rustc process**, and a
**custom profile inheriting release with `lto = false, codegen-units = 16`** for
optimised test runs.

### 6.5 The landing window: bounded, release-aware, and abortable

**The freeze is right, and the argument for it is structural rather than
historical.** One freeze costs each open branch **one** rebase; twenty-six
independently landed theme commits cost each open branch up to twenty-six,
because each moves lines out from under it again — and 26 independently gated
landings cost 26–39 hours of gate time by themselves at the reported 60–90
minutes. That comparison does not depend on how many branches are open.

**And the historical number is not evidence about them** (R3-7). Revision 3
wrote "112 of 150 … so most open branches will have to be rebased", which is the
inference its own step 2 below forbids: 112 of 150 is a frequency over a closed
window of past commits, not a probability for the branches that exist on the day
the window opens. **That sentence is withdrawn.** What decides how many rebases
a freeze costs is the populated inventory of step 2, and nothing else.

**So: keep the freeze, and make the window short rather than the landings
small.** What revision 2 did not supply, and R2-10 requires, is a bound.

#### The protocol

1. **Land ready release fixes first.** 0.4.1 work in flight has priority over
   the split; the split waits for a green base, not the other way round.
2. **Inventory, do not infer.** Before the window, record for each affected
   ticket: owner, base and tip, the items it touches, its outstanding
   acceptance, and its disposition — *land before* or *rebase after*. 112 of 150
   is a historical frequency and **not** a probability for the branches that are
   actually open; look at them. **And the inventory is owner-confirmed, not
   ref-derived**: a branch with no commits on it is not evidence that its owner
   has no work, because unpublished work is by definition not in the refs. Note
   that the in-tree handoff (`docs/handoff/HANDOFF-2026-08-21.md`, the only file
   in that directory) mixes landed and pending states and is not a live ticket
   census.
3. **Prepare outside the window.** §6.2's before-the-freeze column — recursive
   enumeration, the pin subject map, **the class-specific preparation of
   §6.2(b)** (unique subject and loud absence for the named-body and scoped
   guards, complete source enumeration for the whole-source negatives, scope and
   count ownership for the arity sites — **not one missing-subject rule applied
   to all four classes**, R4-4), the child's completion proof under its current
   identity, and §4.4a's diagnostic guard — all land as ordinary reviewed
   commits before anything is frozen. Then refresh
   the relocation manifest against a single green base and record that commit.
4. **Freeze merges that touch the manifest**, not the repository. One named
   coordinator, a recorded maximum duration agreed before the window opens, and
   unrelated work continuing throughout.
5. **Inside the window:** the moves, reviewed as one commit per theme, with each
   move's own visibility, import, pin-path, selector and arity edits in it.
6. **Gate and thaw.** The batch is validated **on the tree it is actually merged
   into**, including after any conflict resolution — independent green commits
   on different bases do not compose. Record the merge base, the result and the
   item map; then each affected branch rebases **once** and revalidates its
   tests *and* its meaningful platform or UI acceptance.
7. **The abort rule.** If the gate fails or the recorded window expires,
   **abandon the unmerged batch, then lift the merge freeze and resume the
   affected merges; schedule any retry separately, as a new window with its own
   agreed maximum duration.** Never land a partial tree to save a deadline.
   *("Reopen the window", revision 3's wording, could be read as automatically
   restarting an expired freeze — which is the one thing an expiry must not
   do.)* If the owner/base/acceptance facts of step 2 cannot be obtained,
   **delay the split** rather than blocking release lanes indefinitely.

### 7.1 How these rows are produced

Two scripts are retained in the tree, and the numbers in this section are their
output rather than prose:

```

```

`bt-app-graph.py` is the reviewer's reconstruction, moved into the tree unchanged
but for its header: tree-sitter over all 102 files, comments and literals
blanked, `#[cfg(test)]` item bodies excluded for the `*_prod` variants, macro

variants. **`root_prod` is the one this plan uses** — it adds an `@root` node for
items owned by `main.rs`, so a module that names a root-owned type is correctly
shown as un-extractable. It was re-run in this worktree and reproduces the
review's `cuts` object byte for byte.

`bt-app-split-table.py` holds the row manifest and exits 1 when the manifest and
the graph disagree. That check is the point of retaining it: the first draft's
table was hand-written, and five of its sixteen rows named only modules that
cannot leave.

**What the generator's exit status means, exactly** (R2-4 — revision 2 sold it
as a broader safety net than it implements). It is **set and `cfg` agreement
between a hand-written manifest and one JSON file**:

| It checks | It does not check |
| --- | --- |
| every module a row names is in `cuts.root_prod.i18n.free` | **row dependency order** — the `ROWS` comment promises it and no assertion enforces it; swapping rows 2 and 3 violates their order and still exits 0 |
| no module is named by two rows | the correctness or completeness of any `BLOCKED_NOTE` string |
| no module the graph frees is left unlisted | item ownership, public API, or whether a row's surface can be published without widening something |
| no module listed as blocked has quietly become free | new-crate dependency cycles, build-script inputs, feature and fixture ownership, test relocation |
| the four test-only files are still entirely `#[cfg(test)]` | the plan's prose, and **whether the JSON describes current source** |

Two consequences the ticket-writer has to hold:

- **Run the builder and then the generator, on the recorded source commit.** A
  green run against a stale `target/bt-app-graph.json` says nothing. After Step 2
  changes module topology, the graph's hard-coded context and external-test-file
  rules must be revalidated **before** it is allowed to authorise a Step 3 row; a
  successful comparison against the old JSON cannot certify a new topology.
- **A passing run is not approval to extract.** It says a row's modules are free
  in one graph of one snapshot. API, features, tests, fixtures, receiving owner
  and build inputs are reviewed by a person, per row.

And its notion of "free" rejects every nontrivial SCC, one member at a time — so
the `{marks, icons}` bundle §7.3 shows to be a real joint move could never be
expressed as two free rows. Grouped-SCC handling is future tooling work, listed
here so nobody reads the current output as a denial that such a bundle exists.

**One known defect in the retained script, to fix when it is next edited:**
`BLOCKED_NOTE["formula_tools"]` says "root `ChromeSprite`". `ChromeSprite` is
defined at `marks.rs:1617` and imported by `formula_tools.rs:43–47`; it is not

### 8.1 Why the sketch was withdrawn

The first draft offered a `Services` / `Turn` / `Intent` sketch and named seven

### 8.2 What "pluggable" may mean here, now that one meaning is gone

*Finding 22.* No first-party crate in this workspace declares a `[features]`
table — the only one in the tree is `vendor/alacritty_terminal/Cargo.toml:40`.
`crates/bt-app/src` contains no `cfg(feature` at all, and CI has no
feature-matrix job. **"Compiled out" is not implementable today**, and
implementing it means a second build configuration the three gates do not cover
— which is the exact shape of the incident `docs/CONVENTIONS.md` records.

So the word is redefined, once, and the plan uses only this:

> **Pluggable** means: a subsystem can be replaced by a test double at a named
> call site, in this repository, and the compiler enforces that nothing else
> reaches around it.

Not compiled out, not dynamically loaded, not third-party. If someone later
wants "compiled out", that ticket adds the feature **and** the CI job that
builds without it, in the same commit.

### 8.3 The prerequisites, before a design ticket is written

1. **Step 2 lands.** A boundary cannot be drawn around a theme whose methods are
   interleaved with twenty-five others.
2. **A read/write/call audit of one bounded candidate**, chosen while Step 2 is
   in flight so §8 is not a standing start. It must cover `Deref`,
   destructuring, aliases, and callees that close over `&mut Runtime` — grepping
   for `WindowRuntime` misses all four.
3. **The contract written before the code**: immutable snapshots with stable

4. **Evidence before the promise**: trace-based before/after event sequences, and
   a working no-op or replacement instance for the chosen seam.

The review's own suggestion for the first candidate is worth taking: **a
toast-only adapter** out of the attention theme is a smaller honest seam than
"attention & notifications", because it separates the ledger decision from the
delivery from the app mutation.

## 9. What NOT to do

1. **No new abstraction for its own sake.** Every trait must name the second
   implementation it exists for, and after finding 22 "a test double at a named
   call site" is the only second implementation this workspace can actually
   produce.
2. **No plugin API in this pass.** No dynamic loading, no stable ABI, no
   third-party surface, no manifest format. Rust has no stable ABI to hold such
   a boundary and nothing in the tree carries loader or registration machinery.
   The review examined this refusal and says keep it as worded.
3. **No behaviour change mixed into a move — enforced by a check that can
   actually see one, and claimed only as far as that check reaches.**
   *Rewritten twice.* The first draft's enforcement was a sorted multiset of
   source lines; it cannot detect a reordering of two effects, and it rejects
   the visibility, source-pin and platform edits that §6.1 and §6.2 show are
   mandatory parts of the same step. The mechanism is now §6.3's paired
   old-to-new item identities with **ordered, literal-preserving token bodies**
   and an **enumerated list** of permitted changes — each with its old and new
   tokens and its reason, not a regex exemption. And the claim is now scoped to
   match: **"no intended product behaviour change, supported by these
   comparisons and these tests"**, never "proven equivalent". §6.3 says what
   those comparisons reach and what they cannot — and why a per-item IR
   comparison is not the missing proof. A rule the obeying ticket has to
   override is a rule that needed rewriting; a claim the evidence cannot carry
   is a claim that needed narrowing.
4. **No shared `CARGO_TARGET_DIR` tricks.** Two worktrees sharing one target
   directory has already cost this project a day. Every worktree keeps its own.
5. **No renaming during a move.** Method, test and module names survive Steps 2
   and 3 unchanged; a rename in the same commit destroys the only cheap
   verification either has.
6. **No `seats.rs` in the same pass as anything else** — for §8.4's reason, not
   the first draft's.
7. **No adding a `[lib]` to `bt-app` as a "quick win".** It saves no compile:
   with a `[lib]`, `cargo test` still codegens the crate body twice, as the rlib
   and as the `cfg(test)` harness, which is what it does today with the bin and
   the bin-harness. And each file under `tests/` is its own binary linking the
   whole rlib. *Two sentences corrected:* `crates/bt-app/tests/` **does** exist —
   the narrower true statement is that an integration test cannot reach a
   bin-only crate's items. And "a prerequisite for some later options" now names
   them: a second in-repo consumer of the app's types, and a public API against
   which §8's boundary could be checked by the compiler instead of by a source
   pin. **Neither is on this plan's schedule, which is the actual reason to
   refuse it now.**
8. **No promising the owner a compile-time number before Step 0 has run.**
9. **No planning Step 3 against any graph but `root_prod`.** Planning against
   the module-name graph is what produced a table with five impossible rows.

## 10. The gate-time model

**Current, as the owner reports it: 60–90 minutes for a full gate, 7–9 GB per
compile.** Nothing here measured that; it is taken as given.

*Measured facts the model is built on:*

- The gate is three commands (`CONTRIBUTING.md:11–19`) plus six scripts.
- `cargo test` codegens and links `bt-app` **twice** — the bin and the harness.
- `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets` runs under
  `RUSTC_WORKSPACE_WRAPPER`, so it has a different fingerprint from
  `cargo test`'s artifacts and reuses none of them.
- **`cargo clippy` is a check-mode command. It emits metadata and never links.**
  So `debug`, `opt-level` and the linker — all of Step 0's items — are codegen
  and link settings that reach **one** of the three gates, and inside it only
  the codegen-and-link half.
- The shortcut scripts forced a full harness rebuild through a feature
  difference (§4.1), and run nowhere in CI. **That one is fixed and landed**
  (`8e6cf0cc`).
- **`bt-app`'s build script was unconditionally dirty in every linked worktree**
  until `5fd6f935` (§4.1), so `bt-app` — the workspace's longest link — was
  rebuilt on every `cargo` invocation. That is the second cause of the observed
  "third compile".
- **Neither of those two rows has a measured size, and this plan will not give
  them one** (R3-8). A before/after figure needs comparable runs on **both**
  configurations, and §4.2 is run only on the fixed base. The rows are kept so
  the mechanism is on the record, not so a saving can be quoted.
- **And the two are not ranked against each other, nor assigned an invocation
  count** (R4-6). They made the same crate dirty by different routes, so their
  removed work overlaps by an unknown amount; "which was the larger half" and
  "how many codegens and links each one removed" are both answers to a matched
  before/after command sequence, not readings of a mechanism. §4.1 and the two
  rows below say so in the same words.

*The model, per step, with each row naming which gate it moves:*

| Step | Gate it reaches | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0 §4.1** the script selection fix — **landed, `8e6cf0cc`** | `cargo test`, and only when a shortcut script runs | removes the identified feature mismatch, which is **one cause of `bt-app` being rebuilt** when the scripts run. *Free, independent of the split, and out of this plan's hands.* **No invocation count is claimed** (R4-6): the build-script cause in the row below made the same crate dirty, so the two can invalidate the same work, and how many codegens and links either one removed is a matched before/after measurement rather than a property of the mechanism. **Not sized here:** §4.2 measures the fixed configuration only. |
| **0 §4.1** the build-script rerun fix — **landed, `5fd6f935`** | every `cargo` invocation in a linked worktree | stops `bt-app` being rebuilt for a `rerun-if-changed` path that does not exist. **Also not sized here, and not ranked against the row above**, for the same reason. |
| **0 §4.3** `opt-level` overrides | `cargo test` | *estimated*: lets `cargo test` reuse what `cargo build` just built. On the owner's build-run-test loop this is plausibly the largest item in the document, and it is the reason it was promoted. |
| **0 §4.4a** the diagnostic source guard | none | **zero, and it costs a little.** It is a prerequisite, not a win: without it no existing test can see a debug-info regression. |
| **0 §4.4b** `debug = "line-tables-only"` | `cargo test`'s link | *estimated*: aimed at the **654.6 MiB PDB** and the link's debug-info work. The image is measured, not predicted. |
| **0 §4.5** `rust-lld` | `cargo test`'s link | *estimated*, commonly large on MSVC links this size; trial only |
| **1** the `i18n` cut | none | **zero.** It buys Step 3. |
| **2** `main.rs` → `runtime/*.rs` | none | **zero to very slightly negative, and the plan says so.** It buys navigation and merges. |
| **3** eleven crates, 31,336 lines out | `cargo test` and `cargo clippy`; **not `cargo fmt`** | **6.9% is a source-line fraction**, and the compile-work gain is a *hypothesis* built on it — half the first draft's claim, plausibly as little as 3.5%, because the extracted code is the simplest in the crate. **Measure each gate separately**, and net off the added manifest and module overhead. and both items that were free have already landed somewhere else.** Step 0's
